\tocsection{\textbf{9/3/26 Lecture 3: }Digression on Product Topology}

\textbf{Question:} Why the product topology instead of the box topology?\\

First, note that we can define the product topology as the one generated by sets of the form $(a_1, b_1) \times (a_2, b_2) \times \cdots \times (a_n, b_n) \times \R \times \cdots$ (with $a_i, b_i \in \R \cup \{-\infty, \infty\})$.\\

Also, if $(S, \sigma)$ and $(T, \tau)$ are topological spaces, then $f : S \to T$ is continuous if $f^{-1}(\text{open set}) = \text{open set}$. Additionally, if $(S, \sigma)$ is induced by a metric, then $(T, \tau)$ is induced by a metric, and the reason is due to the fact that continuity of functions is equivalent to the condition that
\[ x_i \to x \implies f(x_i) \to f(x) \]
which is a concept in metric spaces.\\

Now, for $\R \times \R \times \cdots$, it turns out the product topology is induced by the metric
\[ d(x, y) = \sum_{i = 1}^{\infty} \frac{\abs{x_i - y_i} \wedge 1}{2^{i}} \]
For some reason, this implies that coordinate wise convergence implies convergence in the product topology, but the same idea doesn't work for the box topology. \\


The nice thing about the product topology is that if $K_1, K_2, \cdots$ are compact, then $K_1 \times K_2 \times \cdots$ is compact in the product topology. We will use sequential compactness to show this. First, let $(x_{n, 1}, x_{n, 2}, \cdots)$ for each $n$ be elements of $K_1 \times K_2 \times \cdots$. Now, since $K_1$ is compact, we can pass the sequence $x_{n, 1}$ the a convergent subsequence $x_{n_{k}, 1}$. Now, the trick is as follows: Since $K_2$, we can pass the sequence $x_{n_{k}, 2}$ to a convergent subsequence $x_{n_{k_j}, 2}$. Continue this process, and denote these convergent subsequences $x_{n_k^{(1)}, 1}, x_{n_k^{(2)}, 2}, \cdots$. Now, the trick is diagonalization. Since we have that
\[ n_{k}^{(1)} \supseteq n_{k}^{(2)} \supseteq n_{k}^{(3)} \supseteq \cdots \]
we see that the sequence formed by picking the first term in $n_{k}^{(1)}$, second term in $n_{k}^{(2)}$, etc. is eventually a subsequence of any $n_{k}^{(N)}$. Thus, this sequence converges in all coordinates.
